01 · Why

Why I make things.

There is a particular anxiety that comes with knowing you can build something. It doesn't switch off. It doesn't care that the day is over.

Most of what I make starts there — in that pressure, just behind the breastbone, that whispers this could exist and doesn't. The PriceMart platform, the Build & Bike weekends, the small internal tools that quietly run the day-to-day, the Tiger screen on the wall — they all start in the same place. I follow that pressure for a living.

Twenty years of operating has taught me one thing more than any other: the gap between a hard idea and a working version of it is only ever closed by other people. Not just any people — inspired ones. People who walk in already half in love with the work. The companies, the dinners, the bike rides, the Friday-night arguments about a feature nobody asked for: it's the same thing. Find inspired collaborators. Refuse to waste their time. Make the room around them better than the day before.

That's the actual job.

03 · Trail

The trail behind me.

Twenty years between four cities, building product, hiring teams, learning what to ship and what to kill. The shape of my career has been a slow westward drift across America, then a faster eastward one across the Mediterranean.

  • 2024 →Jávea — building a small portfolio of functional-food and software ideas. Mostly outside, mostly with two kids underfoot.

04 · Family

Tiger, the brothers, and the orange grove.

A few years ago, on a trip to a friend's wedding, I walked through a temple complex in Kamakura and stood in front of a four-panel painted screen of two tigers under a pine. I haven't been able to forget it. When my son was born, I called him Tiger. He moves through the world the way that screen does: present, unhurried, a little bit fierce.

I'm a long way from where I started — California, then Florida, then a slow settling into the Mediterranean — but the people closest to me haven't moved in any meaningful sense. My brothers and their families are the through-line; they keep me honest about what actually matters when the rest of it goes loud.

We landed in Jávea because it had the things a working family needs and nothing it doesn't: a sea, a mountain (Montgó, photogenic on bad days and unbelievable on good ones), an orange grove in the back garden that flowers white in February, and enough quiet to think.
