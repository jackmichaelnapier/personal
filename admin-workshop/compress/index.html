Station 04 · The Notebook Wall

Compress the judgment.

"Follow up on this job" lives in your head as one verb. The careful version is actually five small checks done in order. Write them down once, and the skill runs them every time without you having to decide where to start.

"Be thorough" does nothing.
Numbered moves do everything.
🦆
Otto thinks…

The pattern: 3 to 6 steps, each one verb-led, each one you could ask "did I do this?" If a step starts with "be" or "consider" or "think about" — it's not a move, it's an aspiration. Rewrite it as a verb.

Vague verb vs numbered moves.

Vague

"Be a thorough chemist"

Doesn't tell anyone what a thorough chemist actually does. Every run lands somewhere different.

Compressed

1. Check synthesis. 2. Verify controls. 3. Audit characterization…

Each step is a verb. Each step is checkable. Every run produces the same shape of output.

Try this

Compress this verb into 5 numbered moves.

Write the moves you make when following up on a manufacturing job.

starting point (vibe) 
Follow up on this private-label manufacturing job.
there are many right answers; this is one.
one strong version 
1. Pull the latest update from the manufacturer (email thread,
   ERP entry, shipment tracking). Note the date of the most recent
   contact — if it's older than 7 days, mark the job stale.

2. Extract or estimate the current best-case ship date and compare
   it against the original PO ship date. Calculate the slip in
   working days.

3. Cross-reference any open customer commitments for the same SKU
   (open orders, pre-orders, scheduled marketing). Flag commitments
   at risk if the slip lands inside their fulfillment window.

4. Classify the job: on-track (no slip), slipping (1–14d slip, no
   customer impact), at-risk (slip WITH customer impact), or
   blocked (no update >7 days OR slip >14 days).

5. Draft the follow-up email matching the classification: a friendly
   nudge for slipping, an escalation with customer-impact context for
   at-risk, an immediate-response-required for blocked.

Each step is verb-led and checkable. The classification step is the most leveraged one — it removes the "should I escalate or not?" hesitation that delays every follow-up by an afternoon. Pre-deciding the classification removes the friction at draft time.

Before you scroll on

Take your real task. List the 3-6 moves.

Don't start with the perfect list. Start with "what do I actually do, in order, when I do this well?" Write five lines. Verbs at the front. If any line starts with "consider" or "think about" — rewrite it.
