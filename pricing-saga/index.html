Why we're doing this · The shieldmaiden's question
Too many products on the site are wearing a fake discount: a "regular" price we never actually charged, a "SPAR X%" badge that's been there for months. Customers see through it eventually, and the rules in Denmark (and most of the Nordic kingdoms) are clear about it. The good news: honest pricing makes our value story stronger, not weaker. A real low price beats a fake discount every time.

Old viking wisdom: a sword that says "the sharpest in the realm" had better cut. A price that says "save 40%" had better save you 40%. Anything else, and word travels fast in the longhouse.

WORKSTREAM 01

Flash sale page

Right now, flash sale is doing too much work. Items sit there for weeks. Some never leave. Let's make flash sale do what flash sale should.
PCProduct Catalog Team Due 19 May 2026
Action required
Saga lesson · Thor's hammer
Lightning that lingers is not lightning.
A flash sale is a strike of Mjölnir: short, loud, gone. If a "flash" sale has been hanging on the wall since the last solstice, it's not lightning, it's wallpaper. Customers stop seeing it, regulators do not.
1
Audit the page. Look at every product currently sitting on flash sale.
2
Remove anything that isn't genuinely on a short-term offer. If it's been there for weeks, it's not flash sale, it's wallpaper.
3
Reset the prices on removed items. Copy sales_price into regular_price, then clear sales_price. Example: regular €10 / sales €5 becomes regular €5, no sales price.
4
Removed products live on the Tilbud page with their new single price.
House rules going forward
  • Flash sale means genuinely time-limited offers only.
  • No automatic extensions. When the window closes, the product comes off.
  • Permanent or long-running low prices belong on the Tilbud page with "fixed low price" framing, not on flash sale.
ᚦ
Why this matters · the learning bit
A discount only feels like a discount because it's scarce. Repeat it forever and the brain stops registering it as a deal, the regulators start registering it as a misleading price. Both bad. Keep flash genuinely flashy.
WORKSTREAM 02

Bundles

Bundles are the easy win. Buying three things at a discount really is cheaper than buying them one by one. The discount display stays. We just make the math honest.
PCProduct Catalog Team Due 19 May 2026
Discount is real
Saga lesson · Rope & barrel
Three barrels for the price of two-and-a-half is a real bargain.
Buying three things at once actually does cost less than buying them one at a time, here. So the discount is real, no Loki at work. Show the customer the math honestly and the saving sells itself.
1
Walk every bundle. One pass through all of them.
2
Set regular price = sum of the regular prices of every item in the bundle.
3
Set sales price = the actual bundle price (the one the customer pays).

WORKSTREAM 03

Process update

We want shoppers to see we beat the competition on price. The honest way: show "Our price" next to a real competitor's price, with a source. No fake "regular" price, no fake percentage. Cleaner and more credible.
PCProduct Catalog Team Due 19 May 2026
New labelling
Saga lesson · The scholar's scroll
A price next to a real rival's price tells a story. A fake percentage tells a tale.
The old skalds knew the difference between a true account and a tall one. Sourced comparison: "BodyLab sells the same whey for €25, we sell it for €19,99". That's a story a buyer can verify. "SPAR 33%" against a price nobody ever paid is just a tall tale, and tall tales get the storyteller in trouble.

Sourcing the comparable price
  • Source from the highest-priced Danish competitors: BodyLab, Bodystore, or a specialty store, and other comparable Danish supplement sites.
  • The competitor product must be genuinely comparable: same active ingredient, similar serving size, similar form.
  • Per product, keep a record with: comparable price, source site + URL, date checked, comparability notes.
  • Maintain a dedicated tracking sheet for all 150 SKUs. This sheet is the source of truth for what shows on the site.

Example record format
Flash sale operations sheet
A separate sheet for flash sale. Two jobs: schedule private label marketing items on and off for exposure, and log the realistic regular price for every flash sale deal so the discount is defensible.
  • Rotation covers PL marketing items only. These are PL products we want to give extra exposure. On a schedule, on the page; off a schedule, off the page.
  • Skip the rotation sheet for one-off flash sales, weekend deals, and PL overstock clearouts. They're genuinely unique or short-lived.
  • Weekend Deals go on the flash sale page by default. Temporary by definition, flash sale is the right home.
  • Every flash sale deal logs a realistic regular price in the sheet. The price shown as "regular" must match what we actually charged in the previous 30 days. Don't bump prices up the day before a flash sale.
Q
Quarterly review. Recurring ClickUp task: verify every comparable price still holds. Competitor product exists? Price still accurate? Update the record. If a comparable can't be justified, drop the label until a new source is found.
ᛟ
Why this matters · the learning bit
A sourced comparable price is something a customer can check themselves. That openness is the moat: anyone can claim "best price", almost no-one will show their working. We will. Every quarter.

The five runes · Key points to consider

The principles carved on the standing stone. Memorise these and most edge-cases answer themselves.

The work above changes this quarter. The five principles below are the standing stone: they outlast the project. When in doubt, walk back to the stone and read it again.

1
30-day rule for the prior price
When we show a price reduction, the "prior price" must be our own lowest price in the previous 30 days. Not a market reference, not a competitor price.
2
Don't dress up a stable price as a discount
If there's been no real reduction from our own prior price: no "save X%", no strikethrough, no before/now.
3
Flash sale = genuinely short-term
No automatic extensions. No rolling discounts on the same products month after month.
4
Comparative pricing has rules
Comparing to a competitor is allowed only with a transparent source, a genuinely comparable product, ongoing accuracy, and no discount percentages.
5
Long-term low? Use "fixed low price"
Permanent or long-running low prices are framed as "fixed low price", never as a discount.
